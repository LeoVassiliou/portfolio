Theism Probability Engine

Rate the premises of ten classic arguments for and against the existence of God. The engine uses Bayesian conditioning, with every step shown, to work out how those ratings should move your confidence.

How the maths works
  1. Set a prior. How likely do you think God is before looking at any argument?
  2. Rate each premise from 0 to 100: how likely is it to be true?
  3. Soundness. An argument works only if all its premises hold, so its chance of being sound is the product of its premise ratings (assuming the premises are independent).
  4. Combine. If an argument is sound, its conclusion is settled. If it is not, it tells you nothing and you fall back on your prior. Let A be the chance at least one argument for God is sound and B the chance at least one argument against is sound. Both cannot be sound at once, so we condition on that:
P(God) = [ A(1−B) + prior · (1−A)(1−B) ] / (1 − A·B)

Two properties worth knowing. With no arguments against, this reduces to P = A + prior · (1−A). If both sides are equally strong they split the “settled” cases evenly, so you land between your prior and 50%, with your prior holding whatever weight is left. The model is unit-tested in the repo.

What this model does not do

  • It treats each argument as deductive: sound means the conclusion follows. Real arguments such as fine-tuning and evil are often probabilistic, so read results as a structured way to organise your thinking rather than a measurement.
  • It assumes premises within an argument, and the arguments themselves, are independent. Shared assumptions (for example about causation) would break that.
  • It defines “God” loosely as a creator-level, personal being. Arguments for specific religions are out of scope.
  • The ten arguments are a sample of the debate, not all of it. I tried to give each side its best-known case.
